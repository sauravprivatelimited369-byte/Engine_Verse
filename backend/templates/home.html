The Forge cycle

Heat → Strike → Temper

How steel is shaped — and how a concept actually sticks. The same loop for every topic, so you always know what to do next.

  1. 1

    Heat — learn it properly

    Read the notes: definition, intuition, a diagram and a worked example — written to be finished, not skimmed.

    Browse the notes {{ icon("chevron", 13) }}
  2. 2

    Strike — practise under pressure

    Answer questions and run judged coding problems. Wrong answers are kept in your mistake notebook, not lost.

    Open practice {{ icon("chevron", 13) }}
  3. 3

    Temper — review until it holds

    Flashcards return on a spaced-repetition schedule, and your progress page shows what is actually sticking.

    Review due cards {{ icon("chevron", 13) }}

Why “{{ brand.site_name }}”?

Engineers aren't born.
They're forged — one honest hour at a time.

A forge takes raw metal and, through heat and repeated strikes, turns it into a tool that holds an edge. That is exactly what studying is. Nobody crams their way to competence the night before — you heat a concept, strike it with practice, and temper it with review until it holds.

{{ brand.site_name }} is built around that cycle and nothing else: no motivational wallpaper, no inflated promises. It is free, open-source and tracker-free, and every count on this page comes from the real catalogue.
