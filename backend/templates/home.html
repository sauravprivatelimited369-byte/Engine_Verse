VISITOR NOTES

Field guide.

A few ways to explore the atlas.

How do I explore a sculpture?

Scroll through the four studies, or jump to one from the index. Select “Rotate” to open the sculpture in the studio. Drag left or right to turn it, or focus the viewer and use the arrow keys. Pause to hold a view.

How are the forms made?

Each sculpture begins as a mathematical surface. The viewer samples points along that surface and shades the faces between them. Everything is drawn in your browser.

What does the mesh reveal?

Solid mode shades the sculpture as a continuous skin. Mesh mode shows the sampled parameter lines beneath it. The lines are part of the drawing, not seams in the mathematical object. Switch modes at the same angle to compare them.

How do I make a comparison?

Open the study notebook beneath the studio. Capture plate A, close the notebook, and move the slider without turning the object. Capture plate B and compare the measurements. Add an observation and export the illustrated study sheet as a PNG. Your plates and note stay in this browser; there is no account or upload.

What do the symbols mean?
u
An angle measured in radians. One full cycle runs from 0 to 2π.
θ
The polar angle on the orb, measured from one pole toward the other.
n, N
Repeats around the orb (n) or turns along the ribbon (N).
t, m
Position across the strip’s width (t), and the number of half twists around its centreline (m).
r
A radial distance, in the model’s own units. These are not physical centimetres.
Where can I learn more?

Form Atlas is a Meridian project. Browse the engineering catalogue, explore hands-on projects, or find your study stream.
